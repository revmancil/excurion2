Case study · The founding chapter

How one chapter built
the platform
every chapter needed.

The story of Xi Tau Lambda Chapter of Alpha Phi Alpha Fraternity — 40 years of North Dallas service, a stack of tools that stopped fitting, and the decision to build the thing right.

chapter/gala_wide_shot.jpg
Xi Tau Lambda’s 13th Annual Scholarship Gala · September 2025 · Renaissance Dallas

When Bro. Cedric Washington took the gavel as 20th Chapter President of Xi Tau Lambda in 2024, he inherited three things: a beloved 40-year-old chapter, a Wild Apricot subscription, and a growing sense that the tools were getting in the way of the work.

Xi Tau Lambda was chartered March 3, 1984 as the 607th seat in the House of Alpha. Twenty-two founding brothers stepped forward in North Dallas County — professionals, educators, ministers — and set out to serve. Forty years later, the chapter has hosted fourteen Scholarship Galas, seeded the Sankofa Foundation, and put mentors into McKinney ISD classrooms every fall.

It also, by 2023, had a website on Wild Apricot, a dues tracker in Google Sheets, three group chats, a shared Google Drive, a PayPal account, a Constant Contact account, and — the treasurer’s private favorite — a spreadsheet reconciling all of them.

“We were spending more time managing the tools than doing the work of the chapter,” Washington says. “That’s a bad ratio.”

The moment that broke it.

The turning point wasn’t dramatic. It was a Tuesday night in February 2023, and the treasurer at the time — a working CFO with two kids under ten — had to reconcile the January books.

Dues had come in through Wild Apricot’s payment processor. Reimbursements had come in through a Google Form. Requisitions had been submitted on paper at chapter meeting. The gala tickets were a separate PayPal export. He worked until 1 a.m.

“He didn’t quit,” Washington says. “He said something worse. He said, I don’t mind doing the work. I mind doing the same work three times.”

“I don’t mind doing the work. I mind doing the same work three times.”

— The 2023 Xi Tau Lambda treasurer

What they tried first.

The chapter looked at everything. Wild Apricot’s higher tiers (still didn’t handle requisitions or approvals the way officer culture demanded). MemberClicks, MemberSuite, YourMembership. All were built for professional associations — trade groups, alumni networks with paid staff — not for a chapter of volunteers who turn over officers every two years.

They tried piecing together Notion + Stripe + Airtable. Beautiful, on paper. Impossible to keep synced. And when the 2024 officers rotated in, the whole scaffolding collapsed because nobody wanted to inherit “how the last VP wired eight tools together.”

“The problem,” Washington says, “is nobody was building for chapters. Nobody was building for the specific shape of what we do — a small, volunteer-run, brand-conscious organization with a treasurer, a president, an event calendar, dues, and members who deserve better than a Wix template.”

chapter/mentoring_session.jpg
Sankofa Foundation mentoring session · McKinney ISD · Fall 2024

Building the thing right.

So they built it. What started as “let me fix our chapter site over Christmas break” became, six months in, a real product. One codebase. Multi-tenant from the ground up. Postgres row-level security instead of trust-the-client. Live tenant branding instead of paid theme upgrades. AI-assisted announcements instead of copy-pasting from Constant Contact. A finance sub-portal that made the treasurer’s Sundays possible again.

They put Xi Tau Lambda on it. Then a sister chapter — Alpha Phi Omega at UT Dallas — asked if they could use it too. That was the moment the project stopped being a chapter’s website and started being a platform.

“I never set out to build a company,” Washington says. “But once you build the thing your community needs, other communities notice. And you have a choice — help them, or watch them do the same painful thing you just did.”

What comes next.

Xi Tau Lambda is the founding chapter, but not the only one anymore. A handful of chapters, churches, and member organizations have joined the invited beta. Federation is in development for national orgs that want to bring hundreds of chapters onto one platform.

But the philosophy hasn’t drifted. Every feature that ships to every tenant was built because a real chapter — usually Xi Tau Lambda first — needed it. The kiosk check-in came from the gala. The AI newsletter draft came from the monthly comms load. The moderation queue came from a rush-week social-media incident nobody wants to talk about.

“This is a tool built by a chapter, for chapters, and the day it stops being that is the day it starts sucking,” Washington says. “So we won’t let it.”

“GOODWILL is the monarch of this house. MemberForge gives every chapter a place where that goodwill can actually live.”

— Bro. Cedric Washington · 20th President, Xi Tau Lambda
